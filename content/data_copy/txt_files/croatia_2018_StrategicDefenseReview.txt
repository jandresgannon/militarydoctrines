The President of the Government of the Republic of Croatia Andrej Plenković 

Security and defence of the Republic of Croatia, its citizens, territory, material goods, as well as the Croatian national and cultural identity, are the main tasks of the Government I am heading. The defence sector has a key role in accomplishing these tasks. Since we live in the time of intense and rapid globalization, which is prompting significant economic, political, technological and social changes, the security challenges that the defence sector is facing are now more complex than ever before. Therefore, our defence capabilities should be thoroughly re-examined and adjusted to emerging and rapidly changing circumstances. 

The period following the implementation of the 2013 Strategic Defence Review has been marked by undermining of European stability caused by escalation of traditional security risks and threats and the emergence of new ones. 

Croatia’s international obligations have increased and become more complex. This primarily concerns collective defence capabilities of the North Atlantic Treaty and the development of the Common Security and Defence Policy of the European Union. It is necessary, for the purposes of developing efficient defence of our country and its citizens, to adjust our current defence capabilities and develop new ones. The Republic of Croatia is accomplishing its national interests through cooperation with the European Union, NATO, the United Nations and the Organization for Security and Co-operation in Europe. Strength and efficiency of these organizations is in direct correlation with successful promotion of these interests; thus, fulfilling our newly arisen international obligations is an important task. 

Initial incentive for development of the defence sector in this planning cycle was brought about by the Government’s decisions adopted at the 2<sup>nd</sup> session held on 3<sup>rd</sup> November 2016. Fully acknowledging the requirements and obligations arising from the international security and defence arrangements – the compliance with which confirms the credibility of the Republic of Croatia as a partner and an ally – the defence sector primarily needs to develop in such a way as to be able, at any given moment, to respond to demands for capabilities stemming from own national needs. The defence sector should sustain existing and develop new adequate capabilities in order to respond to threats of military nature. As an important component of the homeland security system, it should, in cooperation and synergy with other elements and participants in this system, improve and develop key capabilities for supporting civilian sector in response to other forms of risks and threats to national security. # 1. INTRODUCTION 

The Defence Law (The Official Gazette No. 73/13, 75/15, 27/16, 110/17 and 30/18) defines Strategic Defence Review (SDR) as a fundamental document in the field of defence. The SDR represents a final report of the process of reviewing compatibility of strategic concepts and existing and planned defence capabilities with defence requirements deriving from the realities of strategic and security environment. 



The Strategic Defence Review is also a source of information on the current state, efforts and intentions in the field of defence for all citizens of the Republic of Croatia, governmental bodies, international institutions, media, economy, academic community and NGOs. As an expression of a wide spectrum of analyses and discussions conducted within defence sector, the Strategic Defence Review outlines recommendations for undertaking measures that will be further developed in other defence planning documents. This document represents a source of guidelines for activities of all components of the defence sector in the upcoming period. It serves as a basis for developing the upcoming Long-Term Development Plan of the Croatian Armed Forces (LTDP). 

The main conclusion of the 2013 Strategic Defence Review was that the Republic of Croatia would develop Armed Forces suitable for challenges of the long-term period ahead of us, capable of carrying out its basic constitutional role of defending the country. 

At the same time, Armed Forces will possess adequate level of readiness, training and equipping so that they are able to share the burden in operations with allies, but also being at disposal to population endangered by natural and technical-technological disasters and other crisis situations.  These guidelines are still relevant; however, developments in security environment require review of mechanism of their implementation. 

Therefore, it was concluded that social, economic, strategic and security changes that have taken place in the period from the enactment of the previous Strategic Defence Review (2013) and the Long-Term Development Plan (2014) require re-evaluation of the situation. Upon implementation of the Strategic Defence Review, the development of a new or revision of the existing Long-Term Development Plan is required.

#### **Concisely, these changes refer to:** 

- Changes in strategic environment that have implications on required capabilities of the Armed Forces. Primarily, in relation to new security situation in Europe and the exposure to new threats and risks from the East and the South; 

- Development of the capabilities deriving from a new cycle of the NATO Defence Planning Process laying down requirements for more robust, more mobile and better protected forces on higher degrees of readiness to participate in high intensity operations. Development of these capabilities requires special attention in planning of the development of our overall capabilities. 



- It poses challenges regarding the size and level of readiness of the deployable forces, types and number of operations these forces should be ready to participate in, as well as capabilities required in order to be interoperable, sustainable and efficient in operations with allies and partners; 

- Rapid development of technology and military equipment continuously generates changes in the character of warfare. This places special requirements before creators of the strategic planning for periodical reassessment of the force employment doctrines and of the status and direction of capability development; 

- Political guidelines deriving both from the 2016–2020 Mandate Government Programme and the National Security Strategy as the main national point of reference for the process of defence planning and defining required capabilities of the Croatian Armed Forces.

# 2.  FINDINGS AND GUIDELINES 

## 2.1 STRATEGIC ENVIRONMENT AND IMPLICATIONS FOR DEFENCE 

\Assessment of the general security context indicates an increased level of uncertainty both in terms of the source of potential military and non-military threats and to manners in which such threats to national security could occur. External and internal security aspects have 



been increasingly interlinked and that close connection is one of the main factors taken into consideration when defining priorities of the development of the Armed Forces and to the defence sector as a whole. 

Even though it can be assumed with reasonable level of probability that the foreseeable future will not bring forth a serious conventional military threat, rapid and unpredictable development of the geopolitical circumstances does not allow us to exclude it entirely. 

Contemporary threats to national security also include indirect operations, not necessarily with intention of endangering a national territory, but with aim to weaken the sovereignty of the state or to influence the state institutions. 

Development of new technologies and their use for military purposes, strengthening of the influence of nonstate actors in international relations and the so-called hybrid activity– which includes simultaneous conventional, hidden, information, and cyber operations – indicate that future military operations will be more complex and multidimensional. 

Achieving goals in operations will demand strong capability of adjustment to changes in multiple operational scenarios in a wide spectrum of operations – from high intensity operations to stabilization operations in postconflict period and peacekeeping operations. 

Challenges the Armed Forces will have to meet require an innovative and proactive approach to traditional concepts of use of force. Planning and conduct of future operations requires approach based on joint and multinational forces. Furthermore, it demands for a greater interaction with other governmental bodies and civil society institutions, in view of the fact that national borders do not represent a major obstacle to multidimensional threats. 

Accordingly, National Security Strategy, due to changed and expanded security paradigm, calls for greater synergy of the governmental institutions, private sector and citizens.

Therefore, defence sector needs to develop new knowledge, skills and procedures with particular emphasis on the development and sustainment of interoperability with allied forces. Likewise, it will need to foster coordination and cooperation with other institutions of the society interlinked within homeland security system. 

### Resilience 

In the stated circumstances of military and non-military challenges in the field of security, in which practically every social, economic or military resource is vulnerable, a requirement for building resilience of the state and the society is increasingly more emphasized. 



The resilience also includes protection of the key civilian capabilities and their support to military activities. 

The Republic of Croatia will therefore develop capabilities of efficient and harmonized operation of different civilian institutions in which sense the future development of the Armed Forces and of other state institutions competent for defence-related affairs will be adjusted accordingly. In this way, it will be possible to employ the Armed Forces as support to society in a timely and appropriate manner. On the other hand, they will be supported by civilian resources – from power supply system, traffic, means of the communication system, health system, economy, diplomacy, civil defence etc. 

### Defence cooperation 

International defence cooperation, as part of the foreign, security and defence policy of the Republic of Croatia, is a strong instrument of the development of defence capabilities, strengthening of political relations with partners, fostering national interests in international organizations, development of good neighbourly relations and 

stabilization of the area of specific national interest, and overall reinforcement of the international position of the Republic of Croatia. 

The main guideline of the international defence cooperation will continue to be on strengthening cooperation with strategic partners, activities within NATO and European Union, redefinition and development of the regional defence cooperation. 

With regard to defence cooperation with strategic allies – including NATO and European Union – emphasis will be laid to the development of necessary defence capabilities, strengthening mutual relations and achieving high degree of interoperability of the Armed Forces. Cooperation with countries of the SE Europe will be directed towards providing support to their defence reforms, with emphasis on concrete activities that could hasten accession to NATO and European Union. 

RACVIAC – Centre for Security Cooperation, as the only international organization with headquarters in the Republic of Croatia, is a strong instrument for strength-

International defence cooperation has an important role in the development of the Croatian defence industry. Therefore, its presentation within bilateral and multilateral activities will be given particular emphasis, especially within various NATO and EU bodies. 



The Republic of Croatia will continue with implementation of the signed treaties, agreements and conventions on the arms control, disarmament and non-proliferation of the weapons of mass destruction and the means of their supply. 

### NATO and European Union 

Membership in NATO makes a significant contribution to Croatian national security. The system of collective defence has the effect of deterrence and in addition to the Croatian Armed Forces it is the guarantee of independence, sovereignty and territorial integrity of the Republic of Croatia. 

Taking into consideration the particularity of NATO’s guarantee of providing collective defence to all member countries, Croatia will continue to make efforts towards strengthening of NATO as an effective defensive and powerful political alliance with a key role of maintaining Euro-Atlantic security.  In that context, strong Trans-Atlantic dimension remains of great importance for Alliance’s cohesion. Having this in mind the Republic 

of Croatia will continue to promote active presence of the North American allies in the European security architecture. In the context of membership in NATO, the defence sector is facing two basic challenges:  credible fulfilment of obligations and active participation of the Croatian defence policy in redefining policies, structures and operation of the Alliance in conformity with national interests. 

Concrete national obligations include the  development of the existing and new capabilities  through the NATO’s Defence Planning Process, contribution to NATO Command   and Force Structure, participation in activities of strengthening collective defence such as enhanced Forward Presence – eFP, participation in NATO-led multinational initiatives directed towards  development and building defence capabilities of deployable forces such as the Framework  Nations Concept – FNC and participation in NATO operations and missions.

As a member of the European Union, the Republic of Croatia has assumed obligation to provide assistance to members states of the European Union in the case of a terrorist attack or disasters, in conformity with the socalled Solidarity Clause of The Lisbon Treaty, or in the case of armed attack respectively. In the context of Common Security and Defence Policy of the European Union, the defence sector is facing new challenges, transformation trends and obligations. The latter is derived from Global Strategy of the European Union for Foreign and Security policy dating from 2016 and the pertaining European Defence Action Plan. An important breakthrough in this area is launching of the Permanent Structured Cooperation – PESCO. What is more, the Republic of Croatia

The aim is to foster and protect common interests of the European Union citizens, as well as principles and values the European Union is founded upon. The Republic of Croatia will actively participate in the realization of the Global Strategy goals, primarily,  through PESCO mechanisms that are, for the most part, oriented towards strengthening defence capabilities of the European Union and stabilization of the European neighbourhood. The Republic of Croatia will continue advocating complementarity and avoiding duplication of NATO and European Union activities. Likewise, it will encourage the inclusion of the Croatian defence industry in the processes of defence capabilities building, as well as the autonomy of the member states and their complete control over the stated processes. The Republic of Croatia views peace building and resilience of the states and societies in Europe and beyond, both in NATO and in the EU framework, as its own interests. Accordingly, only in this context it can realize the fullness of its strategic goals. 

## 2.2 MISSIONS AND TASKS OF THE CROATIAN ARMED FORCES 

The Armed Forces are the cornerstone of the defence of the Republic of Croatia. They are organized, developed and employed for the purpose of implementation of the missions and tasks defined by the Constitution of the Republic of Croatia (Article 7), as detailed in the National Security Strategy. 

Missions of the Armed Forces are the following: 

#### **1. DEFENCE OF THE REPUBLIC OF CROATIA AND ALLIES** 

Defence of sovereignty, independence and territorial integrity of the Republic of Croatia is the primary mission of the Armed Forces. 

17 

**S T R A T E G I C D E F E N C E R E V I E W** 



In its implementation we rely on capabilities of our Armed Forces and other governmental bodies, as well as on capabilities of NATO allies. 

The Armed Forces are conceptually conceived, shaped and organized to be able to, either alone through integrated national response or with assistance of its allies, to deter, block and repel an armed aggression against the Republic of Croatia. 

The Republic of Croatia participates in the Allied strategic deterrence measures and collective defence by its declared and other forces, in accordance with articles of the North Atlantic Treaty. 



### Level of ambition 

The Armed Forces will be able to early identify the threat, to conduct timely preparations, by using own forces to delay and block aggressor’s attack from minimally two geographically separated directions , to ensure timely build-up of own reserve forces and at the same time to receive, introduce and employ the allied forces and capabilities for a joint  action. 

In the measures of strategic deterrence and defence of the allies, the Republic of Croatia will participate with priority declared forces whose capabilities and level of readiness have been agreed within the process of NATO defence planning. If required, it will participate with other available forces as well. 

The Armed Forces will build and sustain the capabilities of control and protection of sovereignty of our sea and air space, in cooperation with other competent governmental bodies and allies. 

#### **2.  CONTRIBUTION TO INTERNATIONAL SECURITY** 

The Armed Forces, in cooperation with allies and partners, will actively contribute to stability of the international environment by participating in peace support missions and operations, crises response operations, initiatives of building defence capacities of partner countries, humanitarian operations and other activities abroad, primarily NATO and EU-led.  This contribution will simultaneously assist the development of the entire spectrum of their own capabilities for implementation of other missions and tasks. 

In the process of structuring forces that are prepared and deployed for such operations and activities, particular emphasis will be placed on those components and staff personnel whose specific and highly valued capabilities make our contribution more visible and more appreciated in international environment.

**S T R A T E G I C D E F E N C E R E V I E W** 



By participating in operations and missions, acting within framework of the international organizations where Croatia holds a membership and through defence diplomacy instruments the Republic of Croatia builds its international reputation, establishes strong allied and partner relations and creates possibilities to influence and shape the policies of international organizations in areas of Croatian national interest. 

Level of ambition 

The Armed Forces will be capable to deploy forces the size of a mechanized battalion battle group, on a rotation basis, to peace support missions and operations, crisis response operations as well as other international activities. 



Organization of the Armed Forces will be designed so that 50% of the entire land forces are structured, trained and equipped for participation in peace support operations and crisis response operations, while 10% will be sustainable in operations. 

The contribution of the air forces, for the present primarily with helicopters, and naval forces will be estimated for each situation separately, with emphasis on forces and capabilities developed in the context of NATO Capability Targets implementation. As to this, 40% of the air force will be deployable while 8% sustainable in operations. 

Having in mind the single set of forces policy, the contribution to EU operations and missions and participation in EU Battle Groups will be synchronized with contribution to NATO-led operations and missions and obligations of participation in NATO Force Structure. This will range from smaller combat modules to mechanized company-level contribution. Such maximum contribution will be made every fourth year. In the upcoming period, the contribution to EU operations will be increased. 

The contribution of the Republic of Croatia to the UN missions at UN headquarters, staffs and UN observers’ positions will remain in general terms unchanged to the current commitments. 

#### **3. SUPPORT TO CIVILIAN INSTITUTIONS** 

The Armed Forces provide support to civilian institutions in protection of the rights and interests of the Republic of Croatia at sea, extinguishing fires in open air, in floods, land and sea search and rescue operations, medical transportation and in other situations in which civilian capabilities are not sufficient, in a manner appropriate to the threat type and level and circumstances in which activities are carried out. Activities of the Armed Forces in inter-sectoral response  to recent „migrant crisis“ showed that their capabilities are available at short notice and can be used repeatedly, even beyond traditional tasks.

Use of the Armed Forces capabilities in support of civilian institutions and acting in synergy with other sectors has been acknowledged as necessary, highly valued and socially desirable mission. Moreover, it proved to be one rational and effective form of using governmental resources in the cases of natural disasters, epidemics, deterioration of energy and cyber security, threats to the natural environment, technological catastrophes and other rapidly developing crises, terrorist attacks and use of the weapons of mass destruction. 

Therefore, the Armed Forces will also develop specific, specialized capabilities in support of civilian structures that will, to the fullest extent possible, be complementary with conventional military capabilities and for which 



analyses will prove are justified to be optimally organized for a long or short-term period within a military organization. The Armed Forces’ units or task force elements are trained for such capabilities and are equipped with specific equipment. 

### Level of ambition 

The Armed Forces will develop necessary capabilities through organic units formed to conduct a basic task of providing support to civilian institutions (Fire Fighting Aircraft Squadron, Coast Guard), additional task (logistic and medical units of the Support Command, S&R capability of the Special Operations Command) or by forming task organized units (primarily from Croatian Army - engineering corps, NBC defence units). 

Organization, training system and standards of equipping with specific equipment will be adjusted to this mission’s implementation requirements. 

Capabilities of the Armed Forces developed for providing support to civil authorities and citizens will be used in international humanitarian operations.

## 2.3 DEFENCE CAPABILITIES, STATUS AND DEVELOPMENT PERSPECTIVE 

The Armed Forces are the main defence instrument of the Republic of Croatia. 

In order to timely and adequately respond to threats, challenges and risks in ever-changing security environment, the Armed Forces must be designed as multi-purpose, flexible and modular forces capable for rapid response and integration with other elements of national power. 



Considering that conducting the Armed Forces missions implies a broader and international response, they must develop, organize, equip, prepare and train for action, together with the Allies on the territory of the Republic of Croatia and outside the country. 

At the same time, the capabilities, the development of which arises primarily from national needs of the Republic of Croatia, must be, in terms of resources and time, optimally synchronized with the capabilities with which the Republic of Croatia contributes within the collective defence system, thus forming a joint, mutually complementing package. 

Development of capabilities which have been, as a national responsibility, agreed upon with the allies in the context of NATO Defence Planning Process (NATO Capability Package 2017) will be given priority. On one hand this demonstrates firm commitment to the Alliance capabilities and on the other it will ensure the interoperability with the Allied forces. 

The services remain a key element of the capability sustainment, while the use of military capabilities is based on jointness through military education, training, exercise and doctrinal solutions. The Armed Forces will continue to join the capabilities of service and functional components with the aim of achieving the synergetic effect in response to strategic challenges. Doctrinal postulates for joint action will be adopted through exercises and experimental methods. Their validity will be tested and the existing solutions improved. The need to redefine territorial deployment of the Armed Forces commands, units and institutions has been reaffirmed in order to ensure military presence according to the specificities of the geographical position and security challenges. Furthermore, this need was addressed to assure a balanced development of national defence capabilities, as well as to facilitate the speed of their employment. Through this process support will be given to quality family life and revitalization of Croatian regions in which the Croatian Army has been traditionally present.

2.3.1 GUIDELINES ANALYSED BY CAPABILITY GROUPS 



### Preparation 

Modernization of the existing capabilities and development of new ones will be approached through the advanced and improved defence planning system which, serving as a link between strategic guidelines and policies, on one hand, and military capabilities on the other, must be based on military expertise and scientific methods. 



The improved defence planning process will be coherent and realistic, ensuring planning stability and transparency, as well as adaptability and agility in the development of capabilities. Content-wise, it will be in time-coordinated manner synchronized with the defence planning processes in NATO and the European Union. Preconditions for this were created at the end of 2017 by the adoption of the Ordinance on Defence Planning in the Ministry of Defence and the Croatian Armed Forces. 

Exercise activities will encompass preparation for the full range of military operations, from complex combat operations to humanitarian ones, as well as for operations in support to civilian institutions in response to natural and man-made disasters. Special attention will be given to exercises focused on joining of capabilities and on enhancing interoperability with Allies. With regard to training, the emphasis will be on activities supporting introduction of new weapon systems into operational use.Cooperation within the NATO framework will facilitate our units to conduct exercises in military formations which we on our own are not able to organize in all their complexity (brigade or fleet level exercises and similar). 

In order to minimize expenditures while developing future military capabilities, it is necessary to include methods of experimental science at the earliest stage possible. Organizational entities will be established and personnel educated for the concept development and experimentation. 

The concept of lessons learned, introduced in the previous period, has not been completely integrated into the system yet. Considering its importance, it is necessary not only to pursue but also to boost its development and incorporate it into the whole structure of the Armed Forces, as well as to integrate it with the NATO lessons learned system. 

### Deployment and mobility 

The Armed Forces will develop capabilities for deployment of forces on the territory of the Republic of Croatia, thus projecting national capabilities to desired areas.

Transport of forces at an operational level will be conducted by utilizing our own or contracted capacities relying on economic capacities of the Republic of Croatia. 

For the purposes of participating in NATO, EU and UN operations and missions and other international activities of the Armed Forces, decision will be reached on the optimal model of strategic transport for each particular situation, considering its specific characteristics. 

The Republic of Croatia has already signed several bilateral and multilateral agreements with strategic partners, which ensure the assistance in strategic transport issues. Likewise, it has joined relevant allied programmes and organizations and signed a number of commercial arrangements with suppliers of land, maritime and air 



transport. In the years to come, the existing spectrum of potential suppliers of strategic transport will be expanded and strengthened in order to ensure timely, highquality and safe strategic transport of forces for every demand and each particular situation. 

### Employment 

The Armed Forces will develop and sustain the capabilities to ensure the conduct of combat and non-combat operations with the aim of defending own territory and protecting national interests on land, at sea and in the air, as well as in cyber space, in cooperation with civilian institutions in the Republic of Croatia and with the allies. 

By its disposition and organization the Armed Forces are ensuring versatility, flexibility and efficiency of joint action, while the efficient logistics support provides sustainability in the conduct of operations. The development of relevant capabilities and conceptual solutions, combined with reliance on allied forces will contribute to deterrence of possible threats. 

Strategic communication, proactive and synchronized functioning of public relations, information and psychological operations with other aspects of the employment of forces in support of operation goals, have not been systematically developed thus far. In the upcoming short-term period, doctrinal foundations upon which modern system of strategic communication is to be developed will be defined. Land forces base their development on guard brigades, with a clearly emphasized priority of reaching the capability of a mechanized medium infantry brigade. Flexible structure of land forces allows for modulation of forces into mechanized infantry combat groups. For the operation in land operational environment the Armed Forces will particularly develop and sustain joint manoeuvre and joint fire capabilities and connect them with other capabilities. 

Joint manoeuvre will be developed through the following capabilities: to conduct reconnaissance, to conceal own forces and intentions, to conduct high intensity operations, to ensure mobility, to secure the key impor-

### Sustainability 

The Armed Forces will continue to develop an efficient, reliable, adaptable, joint and integrated logistics system, sufficient for conduct of logistics and medical support. This implies reorganization and optimization of logistic processes and use of resources, as well as the develop- 



ment of information system for management and exchange of logistic information.

This capability goes beyond 

the Ministry of Defence as a leading institution and implies active involvement of other governmental bodies. Therefore, in the upcoming period the emphasis will be on further regulation of legal and organizational aspects of this complex capability, as well as on the improvement of interagency coordination mechanisms, planning, implementation and administrative procedures related to host nation support issues.

The command system will be rapidly modernized in functional and organizational sense. This will be accomplished by regulation and standardization of command and operational planning processes, as well as by training task organized command structures for conducting joint operations. In support of this process an adequate infrastructural modernization will be conducted, which will satisfy the demand for the mobility of command elements and information exchange security.

Operation planning process will be improved until fully compatible with NATO planning process. Standardized military decision-making and operational planning process in the Armed Forces will be integrated into the mechanism for national crisis management and homeland security system. Inter-agency cooperation will be intensified with the aim of providing an integrated crisis response. The Armed Forces will sustain the capability for organizing, simultaneously, two commands for joint operations for the defence of the national territory. 

Due to the importance and complexity of operational planning process, particularly of requirements for timely planning, the programme and mechanisms for preparation and activation of task organized operational level 



commands will be elaborated. It will be achieved through intensive staff training and employment of existing staff and command organizational elements as a foundation for the establishment of the operation command. 

The process of generating and monitoring the battlefield picture will be improved with the aim of building situational awareness, as well as quicker and high-quality decision making. 

The increase of the adversary’s capability to jam, decompose or destroy command systems by using cyber and other electronic operations, calls for a greater resilience of command architectures, and forces should receive adequate training on regular basis to be able act in the most complex conditions of degraded environment. 

The Armed Forces will speed up development of a unified communication-information system which includes information infrastructure with implemented basic and other required functional services capable of automatic information and interface exchange towards deployable communication-information systems, as well as towards other bodies and organizations in national security system or to allies in NATO and the European Union. 

The development of the consultation capability will improve national procedures and possibilities for distribution and analysis of relevant information created within the framework of the Alliance by expanded use of NATO classified networks. 

### Protection 

Capabilities related to the security protection element will be developed through upgrading of the information security system with an emphasis on the implementation of physical, technical and cryptology data protection measures; through development of biometrics capabilities in cooperation with other state institutions and organisations and NATO allies; through information security system upgrade with emphasis on physical, technical and cryptology data protection measures. 

The information security system in the defence sector will be redefined. One of the important measures will be expanding of the accessibility and use of the classified 

37 

**S T R A T E G I C D E F E N C E R E V I E W** 



information and communication systems – both national and those of NATO and the European Union. The expanding of use will be based on the “need-to-know” principle. The rules of information security should decrease the possibility of information leakage to the lowest possible level, but should not interfere with users’ daily activities. 

Improvement of the nuclear, biological and chemical security system interconnected with civilian institutions, NATO and the European Union, as well as the improvement of the counter-terrorist and counter-diversion activities within the Armed Forces, both of which are important portions of the defence protection element, will be further developed in the coming period. 



The Military Police will continue to develop the capability to breed and train military working dogs as well as to train the working dog handlers for different interagency needs. 

Due to the development of technology and its largescale use, cyberspace is becoming crucially important for the functioning of the state, its economy and society as a whole. 

The concept of engaging the defence sector in cyberspace, based on the concept of homeland security, will be developed and harmonized with all relevant public administration actors, enterprises and civilian institutions active in this field. The study will be carried out to define the capability for engagement in cyberspace in the Armed Forces operations. The study will propose legal, organizational and doctrinal adjustments which will enable the defence sector to give its full contribution to the achievement of the interests of the Republic of Croatia in cyberspace. 

The Armed Forces will act proactively by developing their own cyber defence capabilities, including their own organizational solutions. 

The Armed Forces will continue developing the new and modernizing the existing capabilities for defending their own cyberspace, preventing unauthorised data access in communication and information systems and ensuring confidentiality, integrity and data availability. 

The development of all the elements relevant to the capability for cyber protection of deployable communication and information systems of units in operations will be of special significance. 

### Providing information 

In order to avoid strategic and operational surprises, the Military Security and Information Agency and the Armed Forces will continue modernization and development of capabilities for timely detection of early warning indicators of immediate and emerging threats, as well as capabilities for intelligence data gathering in support of all levels of decision making in defence sector, command system and forces in operations.

**S T R A T E G I C D E F E N C E R E V I E W** 



Therefore, considerable efforts will be made in development and equipping with new systems, modernization of the existing systems for strategic electronic reconnaissance, as well as with radar and _unmanned aerial systems._ 

Military security and intelligence capability will be developed as a unified system within the security-intelligence system of the Republic of Croatia with the primary mission of providing support to the defence sector and activities of the Armed Forces. Its activities are being continually harmonized with the overall capabilities of the security-intelligence systems of the Republic of Croatia, NATO and the European Union. 



In accordance with the lessons learned from exercises and operations, a doctrinal model, organization and training of the military security-intelligence system personnel will be continually analysed and adjusted. 

A more intense development of intelligence activities in cyberspace as a separate operational environment will be conducted. 

The military security-intelligence system will conduct measures in cooperation with the responsible institutions in the country and with allies in order for a timely detection and attribution of various forms of hybrid activities against the Republic of Croatia. 

The Republic of Croatia and its defence sector are exposed to intelligence activities of various organizations. The Republic of Croatia, being a NATO member, is interesting intelligence-wise to regional and global actors. Counter-intelligence activities and security support in the defence sector will be directed toward opposing such intelligence activities and related threats and risks, with a particular emphasis on support for the Armed Forces operations.

### 2.4.1 

### MAN IN THE CENTRE OF CONSIDERATION 

Homeland War confirmed the key role of the human dimension of the Armed Forces that is recognized even nowadays in patriotism, self-sacrifice, professionalism and innovativeness of their members. Therefore, in all considerations of the development of the Armed Forces and the entire defence sector, a man has to be in the centre of consideration, as a bearer of the developmental potential. Continuous priority will be given to the promotion of the military vocation and values of the Homeland War, improvements in living, work and training standards of the members of the Armed Forces as well as to defining new or modifications of existing models supporting these goals. 

41 

**S T R A T E G I C D E F E N C E R E V I E W** 



Amendments or adjustments of regulation relating to defence issues will be carried out. This, in particular, refers to the Law on Service in the Armed Forces – a precondition for further modernization of the system of human potentials management. 

The system of managing human potentials will change from an administrative function into a key management domain. It will harmonize and develop competencies of the personnel with requirements of the working environment, open prospects of the personnel development and elevate the care about rights and interests of its members. 



The Ministry of Defence will be acknowledged as a desirable employer on labour market through introduction of the system of human potentials management in which each individual will recognize the potential for personal development and care about their rights and interests. Development of economy in the Republic of Croatia and a great number of professional options for young people create an obligation to adjust existing model of acquisition and retention of personnel against competition in the labour market. In the forthcoming medium term, an institutionally balanced, efficient and long-term sustainable system of managing human potentials will be established. It will provide support to building capabilities of the Armed Forces, qualitative growth and well-being of the individual and defence sector on the whole. The basis of the newly established system will be transparency that will provide clear information to personnel in terms of their status and prospects, promoting quality. 

The new system implies an appropriate structure of allowances, benefits, remunerations, subsidies and incentives that will be in function of encouraging performance excellence and assuming higher level of responsibility. 

The specificity and high demands of some duties will be taken into consideration and evaluated in appropriate manner – difficulties that result from carrying out of service, real work load of each individual and tailored programme will be developed in support of the well-being and quality of family life of the members of the Armed Forces. New regulations regarding provision of accommodation will additionally provide support to members deployed to service outside the place of residence, including officers employed for the first time. 

An appropriate institutional system of recognizing the merits of military service will be established as a special and highly valued contribution to social community aimed to strengthen social position of the military organization members. Within regulation of the system of military contractual service, the goal is to reconcile a need for steady employment with necessity of a relatively short duration of the military service.

**S T R A T E G I C D E F E N C E R E V I E W** 



The transition system will be formed primarily in such a fashion that separated military personnel – as valuable human resource – whenever possible remains in function of society’s prosperity, even after discharged from military organization. Transition will be enhanced through integration with the programmes of in-service training and employment supported by Croatian Employment Agency and recognition of the military educational and training system by labour market, including obtained qualifications. Given the role of the defence sector within the system of homeland security, specific priority of the system of managing human potentials will be development of civilian expertise in the field of defence. That implies establishment of the special models of professional 



development and education of civilian personnel, and a motivating system of promotion in service. 

It is also necessary to make significant move towards development and sustainment and rejuvenation of the reserve component that is not based on obligatory military service, including contractual reserve. 

In addition to already achieved high standards of gender equality, additional efforts will be made to fight all forms of discrimination and to foster and provide support to female members of the Armed Forces in assuming duties at higher levels of responsibility, including duties abroad. 

### 2.4.2 

### EDUCATION 

Analyses of the past results in the field of defence education point to the need to change the existing model and to establish new educational model for requirements of the Armed Forces, by founding the defence university college as higher educational institution surpassing a traditional framework of the military education. 

That implies redefinition of the role, tasks and organization of the Croatian Defence Academy “Dr. Franjo Tuđman” in accordance with regulation governing scientific activities and higher education in the Republic of Croatia. Such system will also provide education to civilian personnel from the Ministry of Defence and other state bodies, including officials at the highest decisionmaking levels engaged in the defence and security sector.  By setting up a university framework, education will not only receive a new institutional dimension. Principally, it means complete adoption of all standards and criteria applied in the field of science and education in the Republic of Croatia and abroad, and earning recognition of the scientific and educational potential of the defence sector. New educational system will further sensitize the society on national security issues and contribute to shaping security culture.

### 2.4.3 

DEFENCE AS A FACTOR OF ECONOMIC AND SCIENTIFIC-TECHNOLOGICAL DEVELOPMENT 

Defence industry nowadays, as in the Homeland War, has an important role in achievement of necessary military capabilities. 

It is one of the factors of the economic development of the country since with its propulsive and high-technology orientation it encourages development of the innovative environment in complementary industrial sectors. 

National security interests support the development of defence capabilities with maximal reliance on domestic defence industry and scientific community, with concurrent retention of the highest quality standards of the material assets procured for requirements of the Armed Forces. 

In 2017 a goal was achieved – as set out in the previous Strategic Defence Review – that a Croatian soldier, fully equipped with Croatian weapons and military equipment, would be deployed to international peace support operation in Afghanistan. 

Relatively small-scale production, solely for the Armed Forces’ requirements, does not provide economically cost-effective investments in development of the complex, technologically advanced systems. Therefore, future of the Croatian defence industry lays in its expansion to international market where it can be recognized only by high-quality of the products. That process will be fully supported by governmental institutions and promoted by our diplomatic network. 

Participation in international projects of research, development and production will be particularly encouraged as well as business based on licensing, co-production and sub-contracting for a wider circle of users. 

The importation of ready-made products, technological solutions and systems will be carried out with the greatest possible inclusion of the domestic defence industry and scientific-technological base in their development and production. All this will be undertaken within the framework of mutual interest and cooperation with potential suppliers from abroad.

### 2.4.5

# 3. CONCLUSION 

Complexity and unpredictability of the strategic environment, rapid transition of threats in form, scope and intensity, emergence of new non-traditional threats and technological and social trends significantly increase the range of capabilities that the Armed Forces need to develop for the conduct of missions and tasks – from enhanced firepower and mobility of manoeuvre forces capable of participating in high intensity joint operations to capability for joint activities in interagency, international and technologically challenging environment. 



The Republic of Croatia will continue to contribute to the collective defence, crisis management and NATO cooperative security and participate in strengthening the Common Security and Defence Policy of the European Union, contributing to strategic partnership and complementarity of NATO and the European Union. Together with further strengthening of multi-annual cooperation with strategic partners, mechanisms for regional defence cooperation with countries from Central and South-Eastern Europe will also be enhanced. Development of capabilities will be based on the principles of integration and adaptability of the Armed Forces, connecting their activities with other participants within the homeland security system. In midterm period, apart from the development and sustainment of key defence capabilities and acquisition of the new multi-purpose combat aircraft, the introduction of new and improvement of existing capabilities, such as strategic reconnaissance, gathering and processing of information, strategic communication and capabilities from cyber domain will be ensured. Due to their professional competencies, personal integrity and patriotism, the members of the Armed Forces, as well as civil servants of the Ministry of 

Defence are the greatest value and guarantee for implementation of the set tasks. Therefore, particular attention and efforts will be directed to raising the social reputation of the military profession, care for the quality of life and work, professional development, as well as improvement of living and working conditions. The Republic of Croatia will, following the conclusions of the 2014 NATO Summit in Wales, continue the steady increase of defence budget in absolute terms in order to gradually come closer to the targeted amount of 2% its GDP, taking into account national fiscal and economical capabilities and direct 20% of the defence budget toward equipping and modernization, which will ensure further growth of defence capabilities. The State Budget for 2017, for the first time after the multi-annual decrease, provided an increase of defence expenditures; this trend continues in 2018. Regardless of that, the change of cost structure remains one of the key challenges in the mid-term period as a prerequisite for achieving the planned defence capabilities and harmonized development of the defence sector.
